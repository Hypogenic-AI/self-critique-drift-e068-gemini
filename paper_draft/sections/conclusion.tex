\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \benchname, a reproducible framework for evaluating self-critique through both output metrics and residual-stream dynamics. Across \gsm and \csqa subsets, self-critique and revision prompts consistently produced substantial activation drift, while accuracy gains over \direct were not statistically significant in the primary run.

Our main takeaway is that representational change and behavioral improvement are distinct empirical axes. Multi-round self-critique showed signs of structured internal change (improved deep-layer separability and decreasing step drift), but this did not translate into reliable accuracy gains at current scale.

Future work should increase sample size (e.g., $\geq$200 per condition), add stronger controls (equal-token non-critique and shuffled-critique baselines), and analyze token-level trajectories with alignment tools such as SVCCA and Procrustes. Extending the same protocol to additional model families and truthfulness tasks will clarify when reflection-induced drift becomes predictively useful for performance.
