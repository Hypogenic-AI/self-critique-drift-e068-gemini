\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\para{Does Drift Equal Cognitive Restructuring?} Our results suggest that the answer is "no." In all conditions, including the non-reasoning \rewrite control, we observed substantial representation drift, particularly in the deeper layers. However, the \rewrite and autonomous \selfone conditions for the \qwenonefive model did not improve the linear separability of correct reasoning. This implies that representation drift is often a measure of "surface-level" change in the token generation distribution rather than a fundamental realignment of the reasoning subspace. In contrast, \external critique, which caused the most structural improvement, resulted in \textit{lower} absolute drift, suggesting that "meaningful" reflection is characterized by precision in representational movement toward a correctness-aware manifold rather than the absolute magnitude of change.

\para{Scale and Reflective Capability.} The performance of the smaller \qwenzerofive model is an anomaly that warrants further investigation. Its ability to autonomously restructure its internal state during self-critique (AUC $0.550 \rightarrow 0.719$) suggests that the capacity for self-reflection is not a simple emergent property of model scale. It is possible that for very small models, a simple prompt can more easily "saturate" the model's limited latent space, causing a more pronounced structural shift compared to larger models where the reasoning subspace is more distributed.

\para{Limitations and Future Work.} This study is limited by the sample size (60-100 items per model) and the focus on smaller-to-mid-sized models (\qwenonefive 2.5-1.5B). Future research should replicate these findings on larger models (e.g., Llama-3-70B) to see if autonomous restructuring emerges more consistently. 
 Furthermore, the use of RepE to "inject" the external critique direction into the model's autonomous generation could be a promising path toward making self-reflection more robust and reliable without explicit prompting.

\para{Conclusion.} We have demonstrated that while LLMs consistently drift in their representational states during reflection, autonomous self-critique often fails to provide the necessary structural shift for improved reasoning separability. External guidance remains significantly more effective at triggering this internal realignment, suggesting that current self-reflection methods for mid-sized LLMs may still be fundamentally limited by the models' autonomous ability to assess their own latent correctness.
